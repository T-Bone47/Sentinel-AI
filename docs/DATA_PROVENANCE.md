### What is known (before the owner's statement)

- **No record of origin.** The files came with the snapshot the owner supplied
  on 2026-11-13. The repository records no source, author, generator, licence
  or consent for them.
- **"Real" is unsupported.** The README, HANDOFF, IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS, the
  backend's `pyproject.toml` and the ingestion docstrings call them "real
  records". Nothing supports that.
- **Signs of program generation (counts only):**
  - every one of the 500 employee emails is exactly `first.last@` the same
    domain;
  - the 500 distinct names pair just 100 first names with 70 last names;
  - the 10,000 audit-log IP addresses use only 12 first octets, and there are
    exactly 12 locations;
  - every audit-log row's employee ID exists in the employee file, whereas
    real logs contain unknown and service accounts;
  - audit timestamps run from 2026-05-09 to 2026-08-07, and every other date
    also stops in early August 2026.

These signs make generation likely but do not prove it. They say nothing
about who generated the data, with what tool, or under what licence.

## 2. Open risks, even if the data is generated

- **The email domain.** All 500 employee emails use one `.com` domain that is
  not a reserved example domain. Whether it belongs to a real organisation was
  not checked: that would have meant showing it to an AI model or a DNS
  service.
- **Vendor names.** If any vendor is a real company, publishing the file would
  publish invented compliance and risk ratings about it.
- **IP addresses.** The audit log's addresses are routable public addresses.
  They belong to real networks even if they were picked at random.

## 3. Synthetic fixtures — `research/fixtures/`

- **Origin:** `identity-access-v1` and `identity-access-boundary-v1` are written
  by `research/scripts/build_identity_access_fixture.py` and
  `build_boundary_fixture.py` with fixed seeds. Regenerating them reproduces
  the files byte for byte (tested).
- **Identities:** invented `lab-*` names.
- **Addresses:** all 259 source addresses are in documentation ranges
  (198.51.100.0/24, 203.0.113.0/24, 192.0.2.0/24, 2001:db8::/32), apart from
  one deliberately invalid address in the malformed-input file.
- **Labels:** labelled synthetic in their READMEs. Same author as the rules.

## 4. Splunk samples — `research/external/splunk-attack-data/`

- **What:** five files from github.com/splunk/attack_data (Apache 2.0),
  recorded with hashes in `docs/M2_DATASET_SHORTLIST.md`.
- **Contents:** identities, AWS account identifiers and addresses from
  Splunk's own lab, published by Splunk.
- **Redistribution:** allowed if the Apache 2.0 licence and attribution go
  with the files.

## 5. LANL — outside the repository

- **Data:** "Comprehensive, Multi-Source Cyber-Security Events", CC0 1.0.
  Pinned by SHA-256 in `research/M2_PREREGISTRATION.md`.
- **Not committed:** the files are too large. Anyone can download them from
  LANL.
- **What the repository publishes:** counts and line numbers only. The raw
  alert export names accounts and stays outside the repository.

## 6. Frontend mock data — `src/data/`

- **What:** 13 hand-written TypeScript files from the original snapshot. They
  include invented people and company names; PROGRESS notes them on the
  Profile and Settings pages.
- **Not profiled in this review.** Read them before publishing.

## 7. Decisions for the owner
